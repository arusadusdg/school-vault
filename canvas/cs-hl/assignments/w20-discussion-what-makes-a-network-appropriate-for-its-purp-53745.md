# W20 - Discussion: What Makes a Network Appropriate for Its Purpose?

**Topics:** LAN, WAN, PAN, VPN, modern digital infrastructure, network devices, TCP, UDP, HTTP, HTTPS, DHCP, and the TCP/IP model.

**Discussion Prompt**

Modern organisations may use several types of networks, infrastructure services, devices and protocols at the same time.

Consider the following claim:

"The best network is not necessarily the fastest network, but the one that best matches the needs of its users and applications."

To what extent do you agree with this claim?

In your initial contribution, address all of the following:

1. Compare two of the following network types: LAN, WAN, PAN, VPN. Refer to their purpose and characteristics, and identify a realistic situation in which each would be appropriate.
2. Discuss the benefits and limitations of one modern digital infrastructure: the internet, cloud computing, distributed systems, edge computing, or mobile networks. Apply your discussion to a realistic context such as a school, smart traffic system, the WWW, or a distributed application.
3. A school is redesigning its network to support classrooms, staff offices, wireless student devices, internet access and cloud-based learning services. Explain the function of at least three relevant network devices, such as a router, switch, wireless access point, modem, hardware firewall, network interface card or gateway.
4. Describe how two protocols from TCP, UDP, HTTP, HTTPS or DHCP support communication in the school network. Focus on the function of each protocol and where it would be used.
5. Reach a clear conclusion addressing to what extent the suitability of a network depends on the context in which it is used.

**HL Extension Only**

HL students must complete this section. SL students should not answer it.

Explain the function of the TCP/IP model and how its four layers, application, transport, internet and network interface, interact when a user accesses a secure website. Your response should show how the responsibilities of the layers contribute to successful data transmission.

---

**Participation Guidelines**

* HL students complete the common discussion prompt and the HL Extension.
* Post one thoughtful original contribution that addresses all questions required for your level.
* Go beyond definitions. Apply network concepts to realistic situations and explain the reasoning behind your choices.
* Use appropriate computer science terminology throughout.
* After posting your initial contribution, reply meaningfully to at least two classmates.
* A meaningful reply should extend the discussion by challenging a network or infrastructure choice with a justified alternative, comparing technologies, adding another real-world example, identifying a limitation that was overlooked, or asking a focused technical question.
* Replies such as "I agree", "Good example", or responses that simply repeat another student's ideas will not count as meaningful contributions.
* Maintain a respectful and professional tone when questioning or challenging another viewpoint.

**Teacher's Notes - Facilitator Guidance**

This is a discussion activity and does not require a numerical score or Grade 1-7 rubric. Feedback should focus on conceptual accuracy, application to context, quality of reasoning, correct use of terminology and meaningful peer interaction.

**HL Guidance**

The common discussion is based on A2.1.1 to A2.1.4 content supplied for Week 20. The TCP/IP model component is explicitly identified as HL only in the supplied weekly plan, so only HL students complete the extension.

**Indicators of a strong contribution**

* Accurately distinguishes network types and selects examples that genuinely fit their characteristics.
* Balances benefits and limitations of the selected digital infrastructure rather than presenting only advantages.
* Explains network devices by function in the scenario, rather than simply listing device definitions.
* Uses protocols accurately and connects them to realistic network communication.
* Reaches a supported conclusion about context and suitability rather than claiming one network approach is universally best.
* For HL, explains the role of the four TCP/IP layers and their interaction as a model for network communication.
* Peer replies add new technical reasoning, challenge assumptions constructively, or extend the application to another context.
