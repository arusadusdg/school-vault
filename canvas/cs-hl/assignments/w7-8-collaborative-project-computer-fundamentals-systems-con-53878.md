# W7/8 - Collaborative Project: Computer Fundamentals: Systems Consultancy Challenge

**Purpose of the Task**

This task is designed to test and consolidate your knowledge and understanding of the complete Computer Fundamentals unit. Your group will apply concepts from CPU architecture, memory, data representation, logic, operating systems, scheduling, polling and interrupts, and translation processes to one authentic client scenario. HL students will additionally apply the HL-only content on multitasking/resource allocation and control systems.

**How the Collaboration Will Work**

Your facilitator will divide the class into groups of approximately five students. Each group will receive a group-specific client scenario and objective. You must work collectively to achieve that objective. The final response should represent decisions agreed by the group rather than five unrelated individual answers.

* Consult one another before making major technical decisions.
* Divide research and drafting responsibilities fairly, but review every section together.
* Each member must be able to explain the group's main recommendations.
* Record the contribution of each group member in the template.
* Use the same agreed final version of the completed template for submission.

**Submission Instructions**

* Download the Word template provided with this assignment.
* Enter the names of all group members and the group-specific client scenario/objective assigned by your facilitator.
* Complete every section that applies to your course level. SL students do not complete sections labelled HL only.
* Work collaboratively in the same document or combine the agreed group responses into one final copy.
* When the group has finished, convert the completed Word template to PDF.
* Every student must individually submit a copy of the same completed group PDF by the end of Week 8.
* Before submitting, check that diagrams, truth tables and tables are readable in the PDF.

**What Your Project Must Demonstrate (Responses to be added on later sections)**

**1. Client requirements - Explain**

Explain the client's purpose, computing needs, constraints and success criteria. Identify which requirements are most important and why.

**2. CPU, memory and hardware - Explain / Justify**

Recommend an appropriate CPU and, where relevant, GPU. Explain the role of CPU components and memory types that matter to the scenario. Justify RAM, cache, storage and input/output choices. Do not simply list specifications.

**3. Data representation - Explain / Apply**

Use relevant examples to explain how binary is used to store data in the proposed system. Include binary/hexadecimal where appropriate and show how the representation supports the client's system.

**4. Logic gates, Boolean logic and truth tables - Construct / Analyse**

Construct one useful logic circuit for the scenario using standard gates. Provide a labelled diagram, Boolean expression and complete truth table. Analyse what the output means in the real system. Simplify the expression where appropriate.

**5. Operating system - Explain**

Explain how relevant OS functions manage the proposed system, for example memory, files, devices, scheduling, security, networking, GUI and virtualization. Connect each selected function to the client rather than defining functions in isolation.

**6. Scheduling - Compare / Evaluate**

Compare at least two of FCFS, round robin, multilevel queue and priority scheduling. Evaluate which is most suitable for the scenario and justify the final choice.

**7. Polling and interrupt handling - Evaluate**

Select at least three events from the system. Decide whether polling or interrupt handling is more appropriate for each. Consider event frequency, CPU overhead, power source, predictability, latency and security where relevant.

**8. Multitasking and resource allocation - HL only - Explain / Analyse**

Explain how the OS manages multitasking and resource allocation in the proposed system. Include relevant challenges such as task scheduling, resource contention or deadlock.

**9. Control system - HL only - Describe / Explain**

Design or identify a control-system component within the scenario. Describe input, process, output and feedback; identify controller, sensors, actuators, transducers and control algorithm; determine whether it is open-loop or closed-loop and explain why.

**10. Translation processes - Compare / Evaluate**

Compare interpreters, compilers, JIT compilation and bytecode interpretation. Evaluate the most suitable approach for software used in the scenario, considering error detection, translation time, performance, portability and the development context.

**11. Overall evaluation - Evaluate**

Evaluate the complete solution. Identify important strengths, limitations and trade-offs, and propose realistic improvements. Your conclusion should follow from the technical evidence presented in the report.

**Success Criteria: Levels 7-1**

This is a collaborative formative task. The levels below describe the quality of work expected and will guide facilitator feedback. They are adapted to this task from the current Sciences grade descriptors; investigation, environmental and safety elements are omitted where they are not relevant to this project.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Level** | **What success looks like in this project** |
| 7 | Demonstrates comprehensive knowledge and a thorough command of Computer Fundamentals. Selects and applies relevant concepts accurately across the client scenario, including challenging or unfamiliar decisions. Logic, truth-table and technical work are highly accurate. Alternatives and trade-offs are analysed and evaluated thoroughly. Recommendations are fully justified, the different system components are integrated coherently, and the work may show insight or originality. Communication is logical, concise and consistently uses appropriate terminology and conventions. |
| 6 | Demonstrates very broad knowledge and thorough understanding. Applies relevant concepts accurately in most parts of the scenario and handles challenging decisions competently. Technical work is accurate with only minor omissions. Analysis and evaluation are strong, recommendations are well justified, and the proposed system is coherent. Communication is effective and uses appropriate terminology and conventions. |
| 5 | Demonstrates broad knowledge and sound understanding of most concepts. Applies concepts appropriately to most familiar aspects of the scenario and solves most routine problems plus some more difficult ones. Technical work is generally accurate. Analysis and evaluation are competent, although some comparisons or justifications lack depth. Communication is clear with little irrelevant material. |

Applies concepts successfully to some routine parts of the scenario but has limited success with more challenging decisions. Some analysis/evaluation is present, but several sections remain descriptive or incompletely justified. Technical work may contain errors. Communication is adequate but may lack clarity or include repetition. |
